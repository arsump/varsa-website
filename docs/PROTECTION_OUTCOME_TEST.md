# VARSA protection → outcome → cost test

Run November 24, 2026. Public-source desk test. No customer input, outreach, private claims or production changes.

**Result:** a named commercial address could be joined to pre-event protection and reported post-event performance. Building-specific repair and insured loss amounts were unavailable. The financial relationship could be reproduced at cohort scale from published tables. Public sources reviewed did not establish a repeatable address-level feed or commercial demand.

## Published claims comparison: a separate residential cohort

[ALDOI and University of Alabama, May 2025, Table 7 (p. 18)](https://aldoi.gov/PDF/News/PerformanceIBHSFortifiedHomeConstructionHurricaneSally.pdf) compares 1,558 FORTIFIED Roof policies and 1,558 conventional policies for Hurricane Sally. The methodology uses nearby conventional homes within 0.25 mile and excludes tree-fall claims. It reports 75 versus 277 claims and average losses of $791 versus $3,341 per policy. Recomputed from the table:

- Claim incidence: 75 / 1,558 = 4.81%; 277 / 1,558 = 17.78%; relative difference = 72.92%.
- Average loss per policy: 1 − 791 / 3,341 = 76.32% lower in the designated cohort. This calculation concerns loss per policy; the paper's loss ratio uses premium as denominator.

These are historical cohort associations for insured Alabama homes in one event. They are not commercial hotel results, Colorado hail results, projected savings, or individual loss estimates. The underlying ALDOI policy and claim submissions are confidential under the data call. [IBHS designation verification](https://fortifiedhome.org/aldoi-fortified-status-request/) provides a request form; a bulk redistributable certification feed was not identified in this test. Link to and summarize published facts. Obtain affirmative rights before bulk intake or commercial distribution of linked certification and claims information.

## Front Range check

A [Fort Collins design review record](https://www.fcgov.com/historicpreservation/files/1615593447-2020-2-8%20425%2010th%20Street%20-%20COA%20Roof%20and%20Paint.pdf) attributes proposed roof and exterior repairs at **425 10th Street, Romero House** to hail damage. The property-address field identifies Romero House; 300 Laporte appears only as a correspondence address. This is property-specific reported damage, rather than an inference from storm proximity. The reviewed material did not establish the named storm, pre-storm impact rating, verified completion or paid costs. The [city closeout process](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Business/Permits-and-Licensing/Building-Permits/Building-Permit-Process) identifies the final inspection and completion records worth testing next.

## Decision

Proceed with observed outcome evidence as a distinct product layer. Keep the address-level cost claim gated on a signed data source with verified installation timing, outcomes, and rights to join and distribute the output. For a commercial test, replay one storm over a defined multi-building portfolio and count how many independently resolve to pre-event protection, inspected damage and paid costs. Measure the incremental decisions enabled versus a hazard-and-permit baseline. No inference about buyer willingness to pay follows from this desk test. NWF outside-business and IP review remains open.
