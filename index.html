VARSA RESEARCH NOTE · NOVEMBER 2026

What can we actually prove about protective investments?

Property owners need a clear view of cost, available funding and performance. The evidence often comes from different sources.

Our starting library collects published cost examples, program rules and outcome studies. Each record includes its geography, type, date, source and limitations. These records should not be treated as a representative national sample.

Costs vary by scope

A single Raleigh rain garden cost record illustrates one completed project. National attic-insulation estimates are planning benchmarks. FORTIFIED evaluator fees cover assessment services, not the full roof improvement. Comparing these values without project scope would be misleading.

Funding is conditional

Raleigh's Rainwater Rewards and North Carolina energy rebates publish relevant program rules. Approval, property eligibility, qualifying measures, program budgets and timing matter. A location match alone cannot confirm a grant or rebate.

Observed results need context

Published Hurricane Sally research compared insurance claim outcomes for FORTIFIED and other homes. That is useful event-specific evidence, but it is not a prediction of savings for an individual property, nor proof of the financial return of a particular proposed project.

What VARSA is building

A traceable decision record: cost assumptions, funding sources, financial scenarios and a separately evaluated evidence appendix. Our next research priorities are more comparable project costs, dates of program changes, and documented outcomes that can be evaluated across similar assets.

Research note based on the initial curated record set; it does not report new causal findings. Some underlying source entries remain on the independent-review queue. Inspect the source collection.
